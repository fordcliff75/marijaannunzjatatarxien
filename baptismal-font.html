CHURCH HERITAGE · ARTICLE 3

The baptismal font: craftsmanship and parish records

The craftsmen, materials and parish records behind a historic furnishing of Ħal Tarxien’s Annunciation church.

An original English summary supplied for this website, based on Derek Mercieca’s 2017 article, pages 50–51.

The historic font

Craftsmen and materialsPaymentsLater historyDesign detailsSource

Date, craftsmen and materials

Mercieca dates completion to 1754. Antonio Farrugia made the wooden cover, while Saverio Camilleri produced the marble base. The designer remains unidentified; Farrugia is suggested as a possibility. The cover combines a softwood structure, walnut veneer and decorative orange-wood inlay. [1]

Payments recorded in the article

The cited parish accounts record seven scudi and six tari for Farrugia’s wooden cover, and ten scudi paid to Camilleri on 11 May 1754 for the marble font. April entries concern moving marble and the font from Senglea to Tarxien. These are separate payments, not a complete project total. [1]

Patronage and later history

The article identifies parish priest Dun Cirillu Formosa as the benefactor, citing Achille Ferres. It describes the font’s move from behind the main entrance and calls for restoration. Those condition and location observations reflect 2017, not a current inspection. [1]

A detail worth noticing

Its curved front, concave sides and asymmetrical decoration reflect late Baroque or Rococo design. The author suggests some gold-coloured ornament might once have been gilded; this is a possibility, not a confirmed finding. [1]
