01 · FAITH AND TEACHING

A devotion rooted in the village

Our Lady of Christian Doctrine, also called Our Lady of Catechism, is a title of Mary associated with teaching the Christian faith. Madonna tad-Duttrina is a distinct Tarxien devotion, separate from the Annunciation, while honouring the same Virgin Mary.

Lorenzo Zahra’s 1996 history dates Tarxien’s Doctrine congregation to 5 March 1748. Its purpose was religious instruction, and it became a confraternity in 1874. The devotion therefore predates the present processional statue. [A]

Zahra’s account of the 1876 festa describes street decorations, illumination and fireworks before the present statue’s first procession. [A]

02 · THE STATUE

Ġlormu Darmanin’s work of 1877

The present statue was made in 1877 by Ġlormu, also known as Gerolamo, Darmanin (1834–1879). A receipt found by Karmenu Massa corrected the earlier attribution to Darmanin’s uncle Karlu, or Carlo. [B]

Signed on 31 May 1877, the receipt identifies the material as papier-mâché, or kartapesta. Architect Mikiel Busuttil supplied the design for the statue and plinth. The recorded payment, including the star ornament, was 20 pounds, six shillings and three pence, paid in Sicilian currency by Dun Franġisk Feliċ Attard. [C]

Understanding kartapestaPapier-mâché uses paper-based material shaped and bonded into a form, then finished and painted. This is a general explanation, not a verified recipe for this statue. Its exact internal construction and original finishing process are not established by the supplied research.

03 · SYMBOLISM

Mary and the three children

Mary stands with open arms above three children. The published historical interpretation connects the children with the nobility, wealthy people and poor people, expressing equal dignity before God and Mary. This is an interpretation of the group’s meaning, rather than an identification of individual models. [C]

04 · CARE THROUGH GENERATIONS

An artwork changed over time

The restoration timeline follows Joseph C. Camilleri’s 2002 confraternity publication. It records interventions by Angelo Dalli, Francalanza, Renzo Gauci and John Orlando, as well as later furnishings. [B]

The decorated pedestal and display stand belong to later phases of the work’s history. They should not automatically be dated to 1877. The supplied photograph shows an artwork cared for and altered across generations. [B]

Dates and research limitsThe first procession is dated 10 June 1877 in the 2014 account, while the 2002 study treats that precise date as probable. The work understood as approximately 2001 is inferred from the year before the 2002 publication. This timeline is not a complete record of restorations after 2002.

DEVOTION AND RESTORATION

Dates at a glance

  1. 5 March 1748
    Doctrine congregation established. [A]
  2. 1874
    Congregation organised as a confraternity. [A]
  3. 1877
    Present statue made by Ġlormu Darmanin. [B]
  4. 31 May 1877
    Signed payment receipt records the kartapesta statue. [C]
  5. 10 June 1877
    First procession date reported in the 2014 account; described as probable in the 2002 study. [B]
  6. 1923–1924
    Intervention by Angelo Dalli; replacement pedestal attributed to Emanuel Buhagiar in 1923. [B]
  7. 1955
    Gilding by Francalanza. [B]
  8. 1979
    Renzo Gauci repaired damage after contact with an electricity wire. [B]
  9. 1994
    Display stand completed. [B]
  10. Approximately 2001
    Renzo Gauci and John Orlando: strengthening, removal of accumulated plaster coatings and repainting. Inferred from “the previous year” in the 2002 publication. [B]
